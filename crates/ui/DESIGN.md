# DESIGN.md: the desktop app

The design choices for the `photostacker-ui` crate, and the references behind them. What the app does is in the spec, §9 ([docs/design.md](../../docs/design.md)); rules for working in the repository are in [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md); how the logo is made is in [assets/logo/AGENTS.md](../../assets/logo/AGENTS.md).

The crate follows `genetic_algo`'s UI (same author, MIT): its theme, widgets, identity and renderer modules are copied and adapted, each file saying where its ideas come from.

Status marks: **done** is in the code, **planned** is agreed but not built yet.

## Product character
A review bench, not a photo manager and not a dashboard. The stacks are the subject: the centre shows them, everything else frames them, and every action on a stack (confirm, reject, split, merge, write) is one click or one key from it. Native, restrained, readable: light and dark themes, keyboard operation, and a window usable down to 900 × 560.

## Layout (spec §9.2)
One region per module, around the centre:

- **The frame (done).** A top toolbar of 48 px; a left panel, `jobs`, 260 px wide by default and resizable from 200 to 400 px; a right panel, `inspector`, 320 px by default, resizable from 260 px up to 40 % of the window's width; a bottom status bar one text row high (4 px above and below), across the whole width; the centre between them, in the `window` colour, a step back from the `panel` colour around it. The inspector's content takes its whole region (a scroll area that does not shrink), since a resizable egui panel shrinks to its content and would ignore the width it is given; the jobs list's scroll area does the same; the inspector is one pane named `Inspector` for screen readers. **The panels' sizes are preferences** (`panels.jobs`, `panels.inspector`, `panels.tray`), the single source of each size: each frame a side panel is given `min(preference, what the window allows)`, and a width that comes back other than the one given (by more than egui's 1/32 pt rounding) is the user's resize, its edge dragged or a screen reader's step on its splitter, and becomes the preference (tested for both). egui draws a panel at the width it drew it the frame before, clamped to its range, so a panel the window had narrowed would stay narrow once the window grows again: while the last frame drew it narrower than it is given, its range starts at the width given (`sizes::giving`, `src/sizes.rs`, with the settle rule below). Its limit: it corrects a panel drawn narrower than it is given, never a preference lowered by code while the panel is drawn wider (no caller does that). The width a narrow window allows never becomes the preference, so the panels grow back when the window does (tested). No egui private id is used. **Narrow windows:** while the window is at least 900 px wide the centre keeps 400 px. The side panels give way first: each frame their widest is set from the window's width (the jobs list's to what the window leaves it beside a 400 px centre and the narrowest inspector, the inspector's to what it leaves beside the jobs list as drawn), so the jobs list goes down to 200 px and the inspector to 260 px. At 900 × 560 the jobs list is 240 px wide, the inspector 260 px, the centre 400 px (tested).
- **Toolbar (done).** The logo and the name; at the far end the ML chip (below), `New job` (accent-filled: the accent fills only a screen's main action, here `New job`, the form's `Start`, the review's `Write <n> stacks`, the results' `Retry <k> stacks` and `Write the confirmed stacks`, and the questions' actions that destroy nothing, `Download`, `Quit` and `Write <n> stacks`; hover text `Ctrl+N`, `Cmd+N` on macOS), which opens the new-job form, `Settings` (an icon button), which opens Settings in the centre and looks selected while they are shown (a look of its own, `widgets::icon_toggle`: its `surface` fill tinted 12 % towards the accent and a 1 px accent outline; a selected stack's row is tinted 8 % on `panel`, a selected job's row only outlined; screen readers hear it as pressed, tested), and the theme toggle, which cycles follow the system, light, dark, saved to the `theme` preference; `App::new` applies the saved one, and `App` applies a changed preference at the end of the frame that changed it, whichever view changed it. Settings' `Theme` and the toggle show the one preference: a change in either shows in the other at once (tested). **The ML chip** (one button, one text) is the state of the visual check of new jobs (`Controller::ml_state`, below) as a button whose name is its text, `format::ml_state_text`: `ML off`, `ML ready`, `ML 43 %` (the active model's download), `ML: no model` (on, the active model not installed, also while it is imported or its download cancelled), `ML unavailable` (no ONNX Runtime, one that needs a newer macOS, or a build without `ml`). Its hover says what it does, "The visual check of new jobs: open its settings"; when ONNX Runtime needs a newer macOS, the hover first says why, "The visual check needs macOS <needed> or later on this Mac." (`format::needs_macos_text`), and that hint follows on a second line (the chip keeps its two words, which fit the toolbar). It has a chip's look (`widgets::chip_button`: a rounded 1 px `outline`, a dot, the text small in `text`) on a button's `surface` fill (`surface_hover` under the pointer) and a button's 28 px height, so it lines up with the others and reads as one; its dot is `positive` when ready, the accent while downloading, `warning` while the model is missing, `dim` while off or unavailable, so the state never reads by colour alone. A click opens Settings scrolled to the visual check's card (`Controller::open_visual_check`, which the card takes once, `take_reveal_visual_check`, and scrolls its own rectangle to the top of the view; tested). Tests find it by its role, a button, since the status bar has the same words.
- **Window (done).** `main` opens the window at the saved `window` preference (never under 900 × 560), 1360 × 820 when none is saved, and `App::new` applies the saved theme. The window's inner size is saved in points at a zoom of 1 (the size drawn times egui's zoom factor, since the zoom, Ctrl +, is not kept between runs; unit-tested at 1.0 and 1.5), never its position, and only while the window is neither maximised, minimised nor in full screen, so the last ordinary size stays. The size the window opens at is the starting point: only a change from it is saved.
- **Saving sizes once they settle (done; `sizes::Settle`).** The window's size and the panels' sizes change on every frame of a drag, so each is saved (`Controller::set_window`, `set_panels`) once it has changed and stayed the same for 1 s: several sizes within a second save only the last, a size changed back saves nothing (tested with explicit times). The rule is a small pure type fed with `(value, time)`, the time being egui's input time, never a wall clock. While a change waits, the app asks for a frame when its second is over (`request_repaint_after`), or an idle window would never save it; and a waiting change is saved at once when the window is asked to close (tested).
- **Jobs list (done).** A heading, then a scroll area of rows, newest first. A row is one selectable widget named `Job <name>, <status>`: the job's name in semibold, cut short with the folder's full path on hover, and the status chip at the far end; a 4 px progress bar and a small line (`Examining photos: 41 of 100`; a write counts photos, `Writing: 3 of 9 photos`; `Waiting` before a queued job's first tick, `Finding photos` before a scan's) while the job is Queued, Scanning or Writing; `412 photos · 38 stacks` once its scan is done (`JobView::scan_done`) or it has saved its photos. The selected row has a 1 px accent outline and keeps the `panel` colour, so its buttons (`surface`) stand out on it; another row under the pointer takes the `surface` tint. The outline is always drawn, in the fill's colour when it does not show, so selecting never moves the content. **A job's name** is its folder's name; among the jobs whose folders have the same name (a folder scanned again with other thresholds, cameras' `DCIM`), the oldest keeps the plain name and each later one gets its rank by age, shown and named alike: `trip`, `trip (2)`, `trip (3)`; when the oldest goes, the next one takes the plain name. The controller computes the names when the list arrives (`Controller::job_name`), and the status bar uses them too. The selected row shows its actions as icon buttons: Open output folder, Retry (Failed, Cancelled, or Done with failed or skipped stacks: the engine refuses a retry that would write nothing), Cancel (Queued, Scanning, Writing), and Delete apart at the far end. While a job's delete has been sent and has not ended (`Controller::is_deleting`, until `JobDeleted` or a failure: removing a large job's thumbnails can take seconds), its small line reads `Deleting…`, its buttons are off, and no command on it is sent, a second Delete included, which the engine would refuse once the job is gone (tested). The buttons are the row's children, so a click on one is the button's alone. Chip dots: accent while a job runs or waits for its review, positive when done, danger when failed, secondary when queued or cancelled. Nothing in a row widens the panel: every text in it truncates.
- **Status bar (done).** The selected job's totals on the left (`412 photos · 38 stacks`, ` · 3 could not be read` when some could not, ` · 12 unstacked` once its review has read the tray); on the right the engine's activity (`Scanning <folder>: 41 %` while the photos are examined, `Writing <folder>: 3 of 9 photos`, `Visual check <folder>: 12 of 40` for a job in review), and none once the engine has stopped. **A stopped engine** (`EngineStopped`: its worker died) is said by one line that stays above whatever the centre shows, in a `danger` banner: "Photostacker's engine has stopped. Close and reopen the app; your jobs are kept." (the views keep the last progress the engine sent, so it is the line, not them, that tells); from then on `engine_busy` is false, so a job left Scanning no longer holds back the larger preview (tested from the frame's shapes, with the event handed to the controller in a unit test, since no test can make the worker die). The right side truncates. At its far end, always, the ML chip's words as plain text in `secondary`, not a button (one button for one action: the toolbar's), after a `·` when there is an activity; when ONNX Runtime needs a newer macOS, its hover says "The visual check needs macOS <needed> or later on this Mac."
- **Questions (done).** The open question is an `egui::Modal` over the window dimmed with its own `window` colour at 60 %. It is framed on `panel`, so its buttons (`surface`) read as buttons. Its text wraps at 360 px; the cautious answer comes first, as a normal button that has the keyboard when the dialog opens (Enter or Space gives it), and the action ends the row, filled with `danger` when it destroys something (`Keep`, `Delete job`; `on_accent` on `danger` is tested at 4.5:1), with the accent when it does not (`Not now`, `Download`). `Question::DeleteJob(job)` names the job: "Delete <job name>? Your photos and the stacks already written are not touched." `Question::Write { job, to_write, proposed }` asks when the review's `Write <n> stacks` is pressed while stacks are still proposed: "Write <n> stacks now? <m> stacks are still proposed and will not be written. The review of this job ends with the write." (`Write 1 stack now? 1 stack is still proposed …`), with `Keep reviewing`, which has the keyboard (and Esc's), and `Write <n> stacks` in the accent, named `Write <n> stacks now` for screen readers, since the bar under the dialog has a button of that text; its answer writes that job (`Controller::write_from_the_review`). With nothing proposed the write starts at once, and a job written without review (the form's switch) is never asked (tested). Esc and a click outside are the cautious answer. `Question::DownloadModel(id)` asks before a model's first byte, naming every host the engine may reach for it, in the registry's order, each once: "Download <name>? <size> through <first host>, or through <second host> if that cannot be reached. Nothing else is sent or received." (`Download DINOv2 small? 84 MB through huggingface.co, or through github.com if that cannot be reached. …`; with one host, "… through <host>. Nothing else …"; a third host would add ", or through <host> if that cannot be reached" again; "through", since both services redirect a download to file servers of their own). `format::download_question` builds it from the registry's URLs (`models::url_host`, no network; unit-tested with one host, two, and two URLs on one host), the size in `format::mib`'s MB. `Question::Quit` (below) asks "A job is still running. If you quit now it stops, and continues the next time you open Photostacker." with `Keep working` and `Quit`, filled with the accent: quitting destroys nothing, the job continues at the next start.
- **Closing (done; spec §9.2).** `App::on_close_request` holds the rule, and the window's close request only forwards to it (`App::show`, and eframe's `logic`, which runs alone while the window is minimised, so a close from the taskbar asks too): while a job is queued, scanning or writing, by the list or, ahead of it, by the status events (a `JobQueued` counts as queued), or a submission of the form is not answered yet (`Controller::is_working`, read after a poll; tested for a `Start` not answered yet and for a job its events show before the list does), the close is cancelled (`ViewportCommand::CancelClose`) and `Question::Quit` asked, in place of an open question (a delete, a download); a second request while it is open asks nothing more and is cancelled again; the question stays if the job ends meanwhile, and both answers still work. `Keep working` (the keyboard's, and Esc's) closes the question; `Quit` remembers the answer, hides the window (`ViewportCommand::Visible(false)`), then asks it to close, which the next request then does: the controller is dropped, and its drop waits for the engine to stop the job at its next check, which a large decode can put seconds away, so the window is out of sight rather than on screen without answering ("Not responding" on Windows); the order of the two commands is tested from the frame's output. The engine leaves the job for the next start (spec §5.7). A close with nothing running needs no hiding. A model's download does not ask: its part is kept, and the next download resumes it. With no job running the window closes at once. The waiting window and panel sizes are saved on a close that goes through, and stay waiting on one that is cancelled. A hidden window's last pass is eframe's `logic` alone, with no `show`, so `logic` saves them too when it takes a close that goes through (tested: `closing_while_a_job_runs_asks_first`, `the_quit_question_outlives_the_job_it_was_asked_for`, `closing_an_idle_app_closes_at_once`, `a_size_still_waiting_when_quit_is_answered_is_saved`).
- **Toasts (done).** `egui-notify` at the top right, 12 px from the edge and below the toolbar: the level's icon in its colour (information accent, warning `warning`, error `danger`), then the text, wrapped at 360 px so a long path stays on screen; over a popup shadow. They last 4 s, 6 s and 8 s and cannot be closed by hand (egui-notify's close button is a glyph the bundled fonts lack, as in `genetic_algo`). The controller hands the window the notices of one poll as it shows them (`Controller::take_notices`): equal ones (the same level and text) once, and past the first three one more, "And <n> more: see the log.", at the worst level of those it stands for; each is logged at its level, shown or not (unit-tested). A failure repeated for every stack makes a few toasts, not hundreds.
- **Idle (done).** No view asks for a frame on its own: while a job runs, the engine's events and the backend's replies wake the window; with nothing running, no toast showing and no input, it draws nothing (tested). The one wait it schedules is a size waiting to settle: one frame a second after the last change, which saves it.
- **Centre (done).** Until the jobs are read once (`Controller::jobs_loaded`) it shows nothing, so an app with jobs never flashes the invitation of an app without one at start. With no job at all it says "Drop a folder of photos here, or start a new job." above a `New job` button, named `Start a new job` for screen readers and tests, since the toolbar's has the name `New job`, and which opens the form too; with jobs and none selected, "Select a job on the left, or start a new one." (done). While files are dragged over the window, whatever it shows, the centre says "Drop to start a new job" inside a 2 px accent outline, except while a question is open, when a drop is ignored (done). A job in review shows its review (done, below); a job being written or done, its writing and its results (done, below).
- **A selected job, outside its review (done; `views/job.rs`).** A header across the centre: the job's input folder in semibold, cut in the middle (`format::truncate_middle`, to as many characters as fit beside the status chip, the jobs list's chip, with the single character `…`, U+2026, which Inter has, in place of what is cut: three full stops would take the room of the characters they replace; every cut in the middle, through `widgets::fitted`, is this one), so both its root and its own name stay in sight, with the whole path on hover and as its name for screen readers; under it the error the job keeps and its warning (`The visual check was skipped: …`), each a banner in the engine's words as a sentence (`format::sentence`: a capital and a full stop): the icon in `danger` or `warning`, the text in `text`, wrapping, on a frame tinted 12 % with that colour, never widening the panel. Under the header, a column at most 560 px wide shows the state. *Queued:* "Waiting for <job name>." naming the job that is scanning or writing, or "Waiting to start." when none is (the worker is on a task of a job in review), and `Cancel`. *Scanning:* the stage (`format::stage_text`; `Finding photos` until the scan's first report, while it lists the folder); for a stage that reports each photo (metadata, hashes, the visual check's embeddings) `<done> of <total>`, a bar 8 px high on a `surface_active` track, and the name of the photo just done; a stage that reports only once it is done (finding the photos, grouping) turns a spinner instead, which asks for frames only while it shows, so the window idles again once the scan ends. Then `Cancel`, and once a photo could not be read, a card "Files that could not be read (<n>)" counting them all: each photo's name with its reason under it in `secondary`, its path inside the job's folder on hover, oldest first in a scroll area that follows the newest at the bottom until the user scrolls up, then "and <m> more" past the 500 the controller keeps. *Failed:* `Retry` under the error's banner. *Cancelled:* "This job was cancelled." and `Retry`. The centre's `Cancel`, `Retry` and `Open output folder` show those words but are named `Cancel job`, `Retry job` and `Open the output folder`, since the selected row has a `Cancel`, a `Retry` and an `Open output folder` of its own. *Writing, Done,* and a queued, failed or cancelled job once a stack of it was confirmed for a write (a failed or cancelled one under its `Retry`): the writing and the results, across the centre's whole width (below); a queued, failed or cancelled job with none shows its state as above (the rule is the results', below). *Review:* the review, under the header, across the centre's whole width (below).
- **Review (done; `views/review.rs`, spec §9.2, §9.3).** The stacks of a job in review, one row each, then the new-stack row, above the Unstacked tray and a bar pinned to the bottom of the centre, so the tray, the sliders and Write stay in sight while the list scrolls. Until the review is loaded (`Review::loaded`) the centre says only "Loading the review…", never an empty review. A read of it that fails before then is kept (`Review::failure`) and shown in its place, in a `danger` banner, "The review cannot be read: <reason>.", with `Try again` (`Controller::read_review_again`; the results' "Loading the stacks…" alike; tested); once it is loaded, a read that fails is a notice and the review keeps what it shows.
  - *The list* is drawn with `ScrollArea::show_rows`: every row is exactly `ROW_HEIGHT` = 136 px, the new-stack row too, so a frame lays out only the rows on screen (and one past them), and a row never wraps nor grows. `App::stats` reports how many it laid out (`FrameStats::stack_rows`, the new-stack row counted when laid out; `tray_rows` for the tray): with 2,000 stacks at 1360 × 900 it is at most 12, scrolled to the top or to the end (tested).
  - *A row* is a card on `panel` with a 1 px `outline`, `surface` under the pointer. Inside 8 px left and right and 4 px above and below, its first line, 28 px high: `Stack <position + 1>` in semibold, `<k> photos` in `secondary` (`1 photo`), the status chip (dot `dim` for Proposed, `positive` for Confirmed and Written, `danger` for Rejected and Failed, `warning` for Skipped), `needs 2 photos` (warning dot; on hover, that a stack of one photo is not written) for a stack of one photo, which the new-stack row makes (its Confirm stays: the engine decides; its write skips it, so `Write <n> stacks` does not count it), `manual` (accent dot) for a stack the user made or edited, `look again` (warning dot; what it means on hover) for `review_reset`, the ML badge (`ML 0.81` with a positive dot when the visual check kept the stack whole, `ML split 0.64` with a warning dot for a part it split off, `ML skipped` dim, `ML pending` dim after a spinner, nothing when the stack was not ambiguous), and the warning icon in `warning` with the count, in `text`, of the members' analysis warnings and the stack's write's; on hover, each as `<file name>: <warning>`, one per line. At the far end the icon buttons `Confirm stack <n>` and `Reject stack <n>`, always both; the one that matches the stack's decision is filled, `positive` for Confirmed as the chip's dot (not the accent, which marks the selection and the main actions) and `danger` for Rejected, its icon in `on_accent` (tested at 4.5:1 on both fills), so the decision reads without the chip. A line too short for all of these leaves out whole facts, the least needed first: the photo count, `manual`, the ML badge, `look again`, the warnings, `needs 2 photos`; the number and the status always stay (at 900 px a stack with `look again` and an ML badge loses its count and its badge). Under the line, 4 px down, the members' thumbnails, 96 px high, top layer first, 6 px apart, each as wide as its image's shape gives (48 to 288 px; past those, letterboxed in its frame, never stretched), a 144 × 96 frame on `surface` while it loads, with a broken-image icon in `dim` when it has none; as many as fit, then `+<k>` in a chip's outline for the rest, named `<k> more photos` (`1 more photo`); while the selected photo is one of them (reached by the arrows, or by its name in the inspector, below), `+<k>` has the selected look, the 2 px accent outline a selected thumbnail has, and reads as selected to screen readers (tested). Thumbnails are asked of the cache only for a row whose strip is on screen.
  - *The new-stack row,* after the last stack, also while nothing is dragged and when there is no stack: a row of the same height, a 1 px `outline` on the window's colour, reading "Drop a photo here to start a new stack" in `secondary` in its middle (a label of that name).
  - *Names:* the row is one selectable widget named `Stack <n>: <first photo's name>, <k> photos, <status>`; each thumbnail, in a row or in the tray, is named by its file's path inside the job's folder (its name on hover, except while it is dragged); the warning count `Warnings of stack <n>: <k>`. The row's buttons and thumbnails are registered after it, on top, so a click on one is theirs alone and never selects the row, and a click on the row's empty space or its texts is the row's.
  - *The tray* (`views/tray.rs`): the photos in no stack (`Review::unstacked`), in a panel along the bottom of the centre, above the bar and below the list, 8 px under it, shown whenever the review is loaded, also empty, since it is a drop target. A card on `panel` with a 1 px `outline` and 8 px inside, titled `Unstacked (<n>)` in semibold; empty, it reads "No unstacked photos. Drag a photo here to take it out of its stack." in `secondary`. Its photos are thumbnails 72 px high, each in a 108 px wide place (a 3:2 photo's; other shapes letterboxed), 6 px apart, as many to a row as the card's width gives, in rows drawn with `show_rows`, so a frame lays out only the rows on screen: `FrameStats::tray_rows` is at most 6 for 3,000 photos at 1360 × 900 (tested). A click selects the photo (`select_photo`; the selection's rules leave no stack selected), which takes the 2 px accent outline. Its height is the preference `panels.tray`, which has no height until the user resizes the tray: the tray is then 150 px high but at most a third of the height it shares with the stacks (the centre under the header, above the bar), so a short window keeps more of the stacks in view; once resized, the height chosen, at any value (150 px too), drawn between 128 px (its title and one whole row of photos) and half that shared height. Its top edge, in the gap above the card, is dragged to resize it (named `Tray height`, the vertical-resize cursor, egui's hover and active strokes along it), which sets `panels.tray` at once (tested), saved once it settles (above). A window too short for it gives it less, and the preference keeps the height chosen. At 900 × 560, in the `review` screen, the bar takes five lines (the three sliders, then the buttons and "4 still proposed"), so the tray gets a little less than its 128 px (its title and one whole row of photos still show) and the list nearly the whole of its first row (screenshot). With the bar's four lines before the time window's slider, the tray had its 128 px and the list one whole row and the top of the next.
  - *Drag and drop:* every thumbnail, in a row or in the tray, senses clicks and drags; egui makes a press a drag only once the pointer has moved past its click distance (or the press is held), so a click still selects and a drag never selects. A drag carries egui's drag-and-drop payload `DraggedPhoto { job, file }` (`views/photo.rs`), which egui drops on release or Esc; while it lasts, a copy of the photo 64 px high follows the pointer, 12 px below and right of it, over everything, with the popup shadow and a 1 px accent outline, and egui shows the grabbing cursor. Three kinds of target take it, each found by its rectangle under the pointer (egui's hover goes to the dragged widget alone), each, while the photo is over it, with a fill of 22 % accent (`photo::target_fill`; a selected row takes 8 %) and a 2 px accent outline, so a target shows on a selected row too: a stack's whole row (`move_photo(file, Group(id))`), the tray's card (`Tray`) and the new-stack row (`NewGroup`). The photo's own stack, and the tray for a photo in it, are not targets: no outline, and a drop there sends nothing. A drop anywhere else does nothing, and a payload of another job's review (the selection changed during the drag) is ignored. While a photo is dragged within 24 px of the list's top or bottom edge, the list scrolls that way, 720 points a second, so a photo reaches a stack out of view; it asks for frames only while there is more to scroll (tested). After a drop nothing is guessed: the move's result, and with it what stays selected (the selection's rules), comes with the next read of the review; the engine's refusals (another folder, a photo that cannot be grouped, a job no longer in review) are error notices, in the engine's own words for the user, shown as sentences (`format::engine_error_text`: a capital and a full stop). Dragging has no keyboard path: the inspector's `Move to unstacked`, `Start a new stack` and `Move to stack…` (below) are the keyboard's and the screen reader's way to do the same (tested with Enter on a focused button).
  - *Selection* (the controller's, above): a click on a row selects its stack alone; Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on macOS) adds it to the selection as the primary stack, or takes it out; a click on a thumbnail selects that photo and its stack alone, and a Ctrl+click on one does what a Ctrl+click on its row's title does, since thumbnails cover most of a row (tested: stack 1's title, then Ctrl+click on a photo of stack 2, offers `Merge 2 stacks`). Selected rows take a faint tint of the accent (`panel` mixed 8 % towards `accent`), on which the chips' outlines and the buttons' fills still show in both themes, and an accent outline, 2 px for the primary stack and 1 px for the others; the selected photo has a 2 px accent outline outside its thumbnail.
  - *The bar:* the sliders `Hash distance` (0–40), `Time window (s)` (1–60, the form's range, and its text on hover, "The longest wait between two shots of one stack.", one constant for both, `fields::WINDOW_HOVER`: the window bounds the wait after the previous shot, so a stack can span longer) and `Similarity` (0.50–0.99, two decimals), in that order, in rows: each joins the row of the one before it, 24 px after it, when it fits there, else starts the next row. Their rails are one length, as long as the widest slider alone leaves them, from 80 to 160 px. So a centre of about 1,080 px or more (a window about 1,700 px wide, the side panels at their defaults) has the three in one row; the default window's (1360 px wide, the side panels at their defaults) `Hash distance` and `Time window (s)` in one row and `Similarity` under them, all at 160 px rails; a centre too narrow for two (the 400 px of a 900 px window) one a row, their rails shortened alike. With two sliders this is the rule the bar had (side by side when both fit at 160 px, else one above the other), and the order is the form's, the grouping's two before the visual check. The price is a line of the bar: at 900 × 560 the bar takes five lines with "<m> still proposed", four without (the tray, above, tells what that leaves). ## State and the engine (done; spec §4.3)
The views never call the engine, and the UI thread never waits for it, reads a file or lists a folder after start-up: a review edit of a large job takes a tenth of a second or more.

- **`controller`** holds what the views show (the jobs, the selection, the progress and scan errors of the running jobs, the new-job form, the open question, the notices, the preferences) and what they can ask for. It has no drawing code and is tested without a window (`tests/controller.rs`, on a real engine over a temporary data folder). `App::show` calls its `poll` at the start of each frame.
- **`backend`** is the one thread that holds the `Engine`. The controller sends it requests, which run one at a time in order, and reads its replies at the next frame; the engine's wake callback, and the backend's after each reply, ask egui for that frame. A read equal to one still waiting is not queued twice, a slider's newer thresholds replace the waiting ones of their job, and a newer save of a preference (`Request::SavePref`) replaces the waiting save of the same key, in its place, so a slider of Settings dragged across its rail writes the database a few times, not once a frame. When the window closes, the backend lets the running request finish and runs the waiting saves of preferences before it lets the engine go; every other waiting request is dropped (tested). A save that fails is a notice like any failed request, and is also logged with its key, since a save run as the window closes has no frame left to show it.
- **Events drive the reads:** a job queued, changing status or deleted asks for the jobs again, so the list follows the engine without the UI polling it.
- **The engine's words on screen** are sentences (`format::sentence`: a capital and a full stop): the notices of `Error` and `Warning` events, a job's error and warning banners, why a file could not be read (in the scan's list, in the inspector's `Could not be read` list and in a photo's error banner), and any failure shown in the engine's own words (`format::engine_error_text`). A photo's or a stack's warnings are no sentences: each is a line after a warning icon, with a capital in the photo's view and after the file's name in the stack's. A failure of a screen the engine has moved past reads in plain words, with no internal id: `WrongState` "That job is done now, so this no longer applies." (the chips' words), `UnknownGroup` "That stack changed while you were deciding. Look again.", `UnknownJob` "That job is gone.", `UnknownFile` "That photo is no longer in the job." (unit-tested).
- **The models of Stage 3 (done; `controller/models.rs`, spec §8.2).** `Controller::start` reads them once, before the first frame, as it reads the preferences, so the ML chip never shows a model missing for a frame while it is installed; from then on each `ModelChanged` asks for them again through the backend. A `ModelDownloadProgress` moves its model's download on in place, and a read made just before a progress event, taken in after it, never takes the download back (unit-tested). A `ModelFailed` is kept for the model's row until the next command on that model, with whether that command was a download (`Try again` repeats only a download); a removal's failure, which the engine also returns, is left to its event, so it is not a notice too. The refusals (busy, not being downloaded) are notices, as any refused command. `ml_state` is, in order: `NotBuilt` (no `ml` feature), `NoRuntime(check)` (the library missing, or needing a newer macOS), `Off`, then by the active model (the one chosen, or the registry's first when the one saved is not in this build): `Downloading { id, percent }`, `Ready(id)` when installed, `Missing(id)` otherwise. **Where ONNX Runtime is** is the controller's to be told (`StartOptions::runtime`, a `RuntimeCheck`): `main` looks once, before the first frame (`RuntimeCheck::look`: the core's `models::runtime::environment_library`, the one absolute path the embedder loads it from and every message names, and `library_problem`, which finds it missing or, on macOS, needing a newer macOS, reading a few kilobytes of the library's headers and the system's version file there), and the tests and the screenshots say what they want (`RuntimeCheck { path, problem }`), so none depends on this machine's library. A build without `ml` has the same `RuntimeCheck`, over stand-ins of the core's `LibraryProblem` and `OsVersion` of the same shapes (`controller/models.rs`), so the controller, the views and the tests are written once for both builds; its state is `NotBuilt` before the check is asked. The network is reached only by the engine's download, after the user's `Download`; the tests install with a stand-in (`tests/common`'s `FakeInstaller`) and check it saw no call when the switch is turned on.
- **Progress (done; `controller/jobs.rs`).** A job's progress is kept only while something will drop it: a scan's or a write's while the status events say the job is Scanning or Writing, an embed task's while the job is in review (by its events, or by the list for a job no event has moved since). It goes when the job leaves Scanning or Writing, and when a job in review is regrouped, or warned or failed about; a job that starts a scan or a write drops what it had. A visual check that ticks on after its job was sent to its write (Write, then Cancel, while it computed) is not kept: nothing would drop it, and `engine_busy` would stay true for the session (tested).
- **The review (done; `controller/review.rs`, spec §5.4.1, §5.7).** The controller holds the review of the selected job (`Controller::review`): its files, its stacks by position, its tray, the selection and what the sliders show. A job opens when it is selected and its analysis is saved (in review, writing or done; queued, failed or cancelled once its scan is done, `JobView::scan_done`, or once it saved its photos, which only a scan stopped during its visual check does), or when the selected job's status says so, and closes when another job is selected, it leaves the list, or it is scanned again: a scan stopped during its visual check has saved its analysis, so its review opens, and a retry scans it again into a new one, whose files the open review does not hold. Whether a job is scanning comes from its status events, never behind, not from a read of the list sent before its scan started; once the job is in review again, its review opens with fresh reads of its files and its stacks. The review stays open while its job is written. Opening it reads its files and its review (one snapshot of the job, its stacks and its tray); its lists are empty until both answers have come (`Review::loaded`). From then on the events drive the reads: `GroupsChanged` reads the review again, `FilesChanged` the files. A command never reads its own result: an equal read already waiting ahead of it would answer first and absorb a read sent after it, so the command's own event, sent once the change is stored, asks for the read that sees it. Each read of the review also refreshes its job in the list (a Stage 3 warning that a regrouping cleared goes at once). The tray is the review's `unstacked`, never `FileView::pinned`, which a move to or from the tray does not refresh. The sliders show the job's thresholds when it opens: the ones last sent for it while no read of its review has shown them stored yet, else its stored ones (the list's copy of a job is refreshed only by a read of the list or of its review, so a review left before its regrouping was read would otherwise open with the older values). The controller remembers the thresholds sent for each job until a read of its review carries those very values, a request about that job fails (a refusal), or the job leaves the list. After opening, only the user changes them (`set_thresholds` shows the value at once and sends it; the backend keeps only the newest waiting), never a read. A command on a job no longer in review is sent all the same: the engine refuses it, and the refusal is a notice. `write_counts` gives the Write button its numbers: the Confirmed stacks without a file of 2 photos or more (the write skips a stack of one), and the Proposed ones. They, and each stack's warning count (`Review::warning_counts`, by position: its members' analysis warnings and its write's), the results' counts (`Review::results`) and the stacks confirmed for a write (`Review::listed`), are worked out once per read of the stacks or the files, so no frame walks every stack.
- **The selection (done).** The controller's own; it never reaches the engine. It is a list of stacks in the order they were picked, the last being the primary one, and at most one photo. Picking a stack selects it alone and no photo; picking it with the extend modifier adds it as the primary one, or takes it out if it was selected (and clears the photo). Picking a photo selects it and its stack alone, or no stack for a photo of the tray; a photo the review does not show (in no stack and not in the tray) is not selected. The arrow keys step: a stack step moves the primary stack along the list, stopping at the ends, and selects it alone with no photo (with nothing selected, a step down selects the first stack); a photo step moves within the primary stack, starting at its top photo. Merging the selection merges into the primary stack, which keeps its id (the others come after it, in the order they were picked), and leaves it selected alone; confirming or rejecting the selection decides each stack in that order. Stack ids are stable while a stack's members are unchanged (§5.4.1), so a selection survives a slider drag; each read of the review drops the stacks that are gone, then a selected photo that is no longer where a selected photo can be: in the primary stack, or in the tray while no stack is selected (a photo moved to another stack, or to the tray while its stack lives on, is no longer selected).
- **`prefs`** reads the preferences once, at start, one key each (spec §7.3), so a bad value loses only itself. A setter (`controller/settings.rs`: `set_theme`, `set_defaults`, `set_window`, `set_panels`; and `last_input_dir`) changes the value at once and saves its key through the backend. The panels' sizes are also changed in memory by the views while their edges are dragged, and saved once they settle.
- **`platform`** is the desktop behind a trait: `rfd`'s pickers and `opener`'s reveal (`Native`), scripted in the tests (`FakePlatform`, which can also have no dialogs). **A picker gives one of three outcomes** (`Picked`): a path, cancelled, or unavailable. On Linux rfd asks the XDG desktop portal, then `zenity`, and with neither (WSLg, unless `zenity` is installed) logs an error and returns nothing, as for a cancelled dialog; so `Native` counts nothing that comes back within 200 ms (`NO_DIALOG_WITHIN`: nobody cancels a dialog that fast) as unavailable, and nothing later as cancelled; on Windows and macOS nothing is always cancelled (unit-tested with the dialog call injected). Cancelled says nothing; unavailable is a warning notice, "No file dialog is available on this desktop. Type the folder's path instead." for the form's folders, "No file dialog is available on this desktop, so a model file cannot be chosen here." for a model's import, and nothing else changes (tested for each caller). A picker is a native dialog the UI thread waits in while it is open, the one wait it is allowed: on Windows the dialog is modal and runs its own message loop; on Linux the app's thread waits in the dialog call, so the desktop may call the window unresponsive while the dialog is open (the pickers off the UI thread, with `rfd::AsyncFileDialog` and a parent window, are still to do). Reveal hands the file manager the path without the Windows verbatim prefix the engine's canonical output paths carry, which the shell's calls do not take (`format::openable_path`, through the string work `display_path` shares, `without_verbatim_prefix`, unit-tested on every platform: `\\?\C:\x` is `C:\x`, `\\?\UNC\server\share\x` is `\\server\share\x`, anything else is left alone). Reveal can block for long when nothing answers (on Linux it asks the file manager over D-Bus and waits out its timeout when there is none, as in WSLg), so `Controller::reveal` runs it on a short-lived thread of its own, `photostacker-ui-reveal`, never on the UI thread nor on the backend's, where it would hold up every engine request behind it. That thread first looks at the path: a missing output folder is an information notice ("Nothing is written yet: the stacks will go to <path>."), a missing file (or logs folder) a warning ("<path> is no longer there."), a missing folder of the app's own (`Reveal::Folder`, the data folder a start that failed shows) a warning ("<path> does not exist."), and a refusal of the file manager an error. It sends the notice on a channel the controller drains in `poll`, and wakes the window.
- **Start (done):** `main` installs the panic hooks (`logging::install_panic_hook`), resolves the app's folders once, starts the log on their logs folder, then starts the controller before the window, so the window opens at its saved size. A start that fails is logged at error and opens `FailedStart`, a window that says why: in its middle a card framed as the questions are (`panel`, the popup's frame, 16 px inside, at most 480 px wide so a path wraps into readable lines), the toolbar's logo, the sentence, and at the end of a row of buttons `Quit`, which has the keyboard when the window opens (as a question's cautious answer), so Enter and Space quit at once: "Photostacker is already running. Its window has your jobs. If you have just closed it, it may still be finishing: try again in a moment." (`StartError::AlreadyRunning`; after a quit during a job the hidden app lives on until its engine has stopped the job), "Photostacker cannot find a folder for its data." (`NoAppDirs`), "Photostacker cannot start one of its threads: <error>." (`Thread`: the thread that holds the engine), and for a database that cannot be opened (`Engine`, `Io`, which carry the database's path) "Photostacker cannot open its database at <path>: <error>." (when the error's words already name the path, as SQLite's "unable to open database file: <path>" do, "Photostacker cannot open its database: <error>.", so the path is named once; no second full stop after an error that ends with one; unit-tested) with `Open the folder` before `Quit`, which shows the data folder (`Reveal::Folder`: a folder never made is a warning, "<path> does not exist.") through `reveal_in_the_background` (the controller's reveal thread, `photostacker-ui-reveal`, usable without a controller), so the window never waits for the file manager; why it could not, under the buttons. **No heading:** every sentence already begins with "Photostacker …", so a heading "Photostacker cannot start" said it twice, and contradicted "is already running"; the logo and the window's title name the app. The error's own words are kept whole, for the log and for a search. `FailedStart` is its own module, `failed_start.rs` (tested: `a_second_app_on_the_same_data_says_it_is_already_running`, `a_database_that_cannot_be_opened_offers_its_folder`).
- **The log (done; `logging.rs`, spec §7.1).** A file a day in the logs folder, `photostacker.<date>.log` (UTC, `tracing-appender`'s naming), the last 7 kept, written by the appender's own thread (`non_blocking`; its guard lives until `main` returns, and writes out what waits); also stderr in a debug build, or when there is no file. The filter is `RUST_LOG`, else `info,rawler=off`; an unreadable `RUST_LOG` falls back and is logged as a warning. `log` records are bridged (`LogTracer`), as in the CLI: a line of one reads `log: <message> log.target=<crate> …`. The first lines: the version, the data and logs folders, and the `ml` and `heic` features. **The panic line:** a panic outside the core's guard is logged at error, `panicked: <message>` with its location (not its thread's name, so the hook adds no `thread::current` call, which can panic while a thread's locals are destroyed, where a panic in a hook aborts; the logging call keeps a thread-local buffer of its own, so a residual risk during a thread's teardown remains, accepted), then reaches the hook it wraps (stderr); the core's hook (`guard::install_panic_hook`) is installed after it, around it, so it sees each panic first and keeps one inside `guard::catch` at debug. **An ERROR `panicked:` line is not always a crash:** a panic in rawler's parallel code while a stack is written runs on a global rayon worker, outside any `guard::catch` (spec §4.3), so it is logged at error, and the engine then reports that one stack as failed and goes on. A logs folder that cannot be used (or no app folders at all) leaves the log on stderr, and the app starts. The pieces (`file_writer`, the filter, the subscriber) are tested with a subscriber of the test's own, never the process's.

## Thumbnails (done; spec §9.1)
The engine writes each file's review thumbnail as a JPEG of 256 px on the long side, under `<thumbs>/<job id>/`, and the review names its path (`FileView::thumb`). `thumbs::ThumbCache`, which `App` owns, turns them into egui textures, which the review's rows draw.

- **Off the UI thread.** `get` looks only in memory: a thumbnail not there is `Loading`, and is asked of one loader thread, `photostacker-ui-thumbs`, which reads and decodes the JPEG into pixels and requests a repaint; `poll`, at the start of each frame, makes the textures (linear filtering, the image's own size). A file that is gone or does not decode (a decoder's panic included) is `Missing`, a placeholder, and is not asked for again until its job is left. The loader holds no texture and ends with the cache; with nothing to do it waits, so the window stays idle (tested).
- **Three limits.** At most `TEXTURES` = 1024 textures are held, the least recently used going first. It must exceed what one frame shows, or a visible texture would be evicted and loaded again for ever, and nothing else enforces it. A 4K window (3840 × 2160 at a zoom of 1, the side panels at their default widths) leaves the review a strip about 3,230 px wide: at the narrowest a thumbnail is drawn (48 px, plus its 6 px gap) a stack's row shows 59, and 136 px rows (with egui's 4 px between them) in the height the default tray leaves give about 14 rows laid out, about 830 thumbnails, with about 56 more in the tray (28 to a row); the tray at its largest, half the shared height, gives about 360 in it and 530 in the rows. So about 900 at most, which 384 did not cover: 1024 does. Memory: about 180 MB for 3:2 photos (a 256 × 171 texture each, 175 KB), less for the narrow ones that fill a screen (about 128 KB), and at most 256 MiB, which only square photos reach (256 × 256, 256 KiB each); the failures remembered are bounded the same way. The count holds at a zoom of 1: zoomed out, a 4K window can show more than 1024 thumbnails, and the least recently used are then loaded again as they come back into view. At most `QUEUE` = 256 requests wait for the loader. At most `DECODED` = 64 images are decoded, or being decoded, and not yet textures (about 16 MB): the loader waits for `poll` to take some before it decodes more, so slow frames that each show many new cards cannot pile pixels up; each image `poll` lets go of (uploaded, unreadable, or of a job left) and each one `forget_job` drops frees the loader, and dropping the cache stops it even while it waits.
- **Newest first.** The loader takes the request asked last: while the list scrolls, what is on screen now loads before what scrolled past. When the queue is full the oldest request is forgotten, so a later `get` asks again if its card is still visible. A path waiting or being decoded is not asked twice.
- **At most 32 uploads a frame** (`UPLOADS_PER_FRAME`), so a burst of finished images cannot stall a frame; `poll` asks for another frame while some are left.
- **Views ask only for visible thumbnails:** they call `get` only for a card whose rectangle is visible (`ui.is_rect_visible`).
- **One job at a time.** When the selected job changes, or is deleted, `App` calls `forget_job` with the folder of the job it leaves (`Controller::job_thumbs`, the engine's own rule, `photostacker_core::thumbs::job_thumbs_dir`): its textures, failures and waiting requests go, by comparing paths, without a file-system call, and an image of it still being decoded is dropped when it arrives.

## The inspector's previews and predictions (done; `inspect.rs`, spec §9.2)
The inspector's two slow reads, the larger preview of the selected photo (`decode::thumbnail` at 1,024 px on the long side, never enlarged) and what the selected stack will be written as (`write::predict_stack`), run on one thread, `photostacker-ui-inspect`, which `App` owns. `Inspect::preview` and `Inspect::prediction` look only in memory and queue a request; `Inspect::poll`, at the start of each frame, takes in the results (making the previews' textures). No file is touched on the UI thread.

- **Latest wins.** At most one preview and one prediction wait; a newer request of a kind replaces the one waiting. A prediction being made is stopped through `predict_stack`'s stop flag when another is asked for, or when a frame passes without asking for it (another selection); a waiting request no frame asked for is dropped. A preview decode cannot be stopped: its result is kept even if nobody looks at it now. The thread takes a waiting prediction before a waiting preview: it only reads headers.
- **What a result answers.** A preview is filed under its job and path; a prediction under its job, its stack's members in layer order, the job's format and RAW tone. So a late result never shows under another selection, and a stack whose members change (a move, a swap) is asked for again. The prediction is given the same facts the writer is: each member's own path, kind and EXIF from the analysis, in layer order.
- **Kept.** The last 8 previews (as textures, about 32 MB at most) and the last 32 predictions, the least recently used going first. A failure (a photo that does not decode, a decoder's or a probe's panic, caught), and a prediction with a photo that could not be read (on a drive that is away, "None of its photos can be read." among them), is kept only while it is shown, with its reason: the poll after a frame that did not show it drops it, so the next time its photo or stack is selected it is asked for again, and a drive that came back is read (unit-tested); a stopped prediction is not kept.
- **Memory.** A preview is one full decode, outside the engine's 2 GiB memory budget: one at a time bounds it, and none is asked for while the engine is busy (above), so a write's full-resolution layer never has a preview beside it ("one full-resolution layer in memory at a time"). The one overlap accepted: a preview decode already running when a job starts cannot be interrupted, and finishes. RAW previews from the embedded JPEG, or a permit of the engine's budget, would lift the wait (carried over to M5). A preview of 1,024 px is never larger than the GPU's largest texture; a larger one would be a failure.
- **Drop.** Dropping it sets the quit flag and the running prediction's stop flag and wakes the thread, but does not wait for a decode in flight (a RAW can take seconds and must not hold the window open): the thread ends by itself once it is done, holding nothing but its queue and the context it repaints. With nothing asked, it waits, so the window stays idle.

Every screen is told ONNX Runtime is at a made-up path, found except in `models-failed` (missing) and `needs-macos`. A screen is taken after its frames once the inspector has what it asked for (`App::inspecting`), or after 30 s all the same. ## Dependencies and the egui version
The UI is on egui / eframe 0.37. Before adding an egui crate, check its egui requirement on crates.io: a crate built for another egui version brings a second, incompatible copy of egui.

| Crate | egui | Use |
|---|---|---|
| eframe 0.37 | 0.37 | the window, OpenGL or wgpu, accesskit, the system clipboard (`clipboard`: arboard, MIT OR Apache-2.0, which on Windows brings clipboard-win and error-code, BSL-1.0, a permissive licence that asks no notice of a binary; and on Wayland smithay-clipboard, MIT, with its tree, MIT; a path pastes into a field from another program, and the About card's addresses copy out; checked by hand, since a test can send a paste event but not another program's clipboard) (in use) |
| egui_material_icons 0.9 | 0.37 | UI icons (in use) |
| egui_kittest 0.37 | 0.37 | interaction tests, dev only (in use: `tests/interaction.rs`) |
| egui-notify 0.24 | 0.37 | toasts (in use) |
| rfd 0.18 | none | folder and file pickers, through the XDG portal on Linux, or `zenity` without one (in use: the new-job form's folders, a model's import) |
| opener 0.9 | none | reveal in the file manager (in use: a job's output folder) |
| lru 0.19 | none | the thumbnail textures, least recently used first out (in use) |
| image 0.25, `jpeg` | none | decodes the review thumbnails on the loader thread (in use) |
| tracing-appender 0.2 | none | the log files, a file a day (in use: `logging.rs`) |

**fastframe is a reference, not a dependency.** Its crates pin egui 0.36, so they cannot join an egui 0.37 build. Its code is MIT: small pure modules are copied with attribution in the file header. `theme.rs` follows fastframe's colour-role recipe and ZapFast's mapping onto egui closely enough to carry their notice, in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`.
